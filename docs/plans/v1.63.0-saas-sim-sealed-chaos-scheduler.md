## Why sealed

The platform handles faults it has met before very well: memory holds dozens of incident references.
The question is whether it handles one nobody chose for it. So neither Claude nor the operator may know
which fault fires when.

## Design

- **Catalog.** `faults.yaml` lists each fault with its blast radius, its reversal command, and the read-only
  probe that proves the injection took effect. The starting catalog:
  - stop one k3s agent node (not the server);
  - expire the sim app's ESO-synced credential early;
  - remove one rule from the sim app's Vault policy;
  - fill the sim namespace's PVC to 95%;
  - break DNS for the sim service, by scaling its Service endpoints to zero;
  - kill the Grafana pod;
  - pause the Alertmanager Slack/email route, a fault in detection itself.
- **Schedule.** One fault every 2–3 days at a random time, 10–12 in 30 days. The scheduler draws the
  schedule from a random seed and commits only `sha256(seed || schedule)` at run start. The seed and the
  schedule are kept in a Keychain item that only the scheduler's LaunchAgent reads. They are revealed
  in the final report, which checks the commitment.
- **Safety.** Never Hostinger. Never the Vault server, its unseal keys, the Keychain or ArgoCD's own
  credentials. Never a fault without a tested reversal. One fault active at a time. The global abort,
  `make saas-sim-abort`, reverses the active fault and disables the scheduler. A fault auto-reverses after
  4 h if nobody has recovered it: that bounds the harm, and the breach still counts.
- **Proof of external behaviour.** Before dispatch, each injector's effect and its reversal are captured once
  live on the deployed versions (host, date, versions), per the 2026-11-07 rule. The offline tests use
  those captures as stubs.

## Acceptance

```yaml
interface:
  - "bin/k3dm-saas-sim chaos plan --seed-file F --days 30 --dry-run"
  - "bin/k3dm-saas-sim chaos verify --commitment H --seed-file F"
  - "bin/k3dm-saas-sim chaos inject|reverse --fault ID --dry-run"
  - "make saas-sim-abort"
must:
  - id: M1
    text: "chaos plan yields 10-12 faults over 30 days, spaced at least 48 h apart, with one active at a time."
  - id: M2
    text: "chaos verify accepts the matching seed and rejects any other seed or schedule."
  - id: M3
    text: "every catalog fault has a reversal, and reverse after inject restores the captured pre-fault state in the stub."
  - id: M4
    text: "a fault left active for 4 h is reversed automatically and recorded as a breach."
must_not:
  - id: N1
    text: "any injector targets a host or resource outside the allowlist: Hostinger, Vault server, Keychain, ArgoCD credentials (red with the allowlist check removed)."
  - id: N2
    text: "the schedule or seed is readable from git, logs, the dashboard or Claude's session before the run ends."
  - id: N3
    text: "a second fault is injected while one is still active."
  - id: N4
    text: "make saas-sim-abort leaves the scheduler enabled or the active fault in place."
evidence:
  - id: E1
    proves: [M1, M2, M3, M4, N1, N3, N4]
    kind: offline
    text: "pytest with captured-output stubs; N1 and N3 shown red"
  - id: E2
    proves: [N2]
    kind: offline
    text: "grep of repo, logs and dashboard JSON for the seed after a dry-run plan"
  - id: E3
    proves: [M3, N4]
    kind: live
    text: "operator: one inject and abort of each fault during the pilot, with probe output before and after"
```
