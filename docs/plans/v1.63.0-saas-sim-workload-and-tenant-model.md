## The question

> Could k3d-manager support the infrastructure operations of a simulated 30-person SaaS company for 30
> consecutive days?

The experiment (operator, 2026-11-07) answers it with fixed targets, a failure condition stated in
advance and unannounced faults. The answer is valuable either way: "no, because X" names the next
platform investment. This plan builds the company being simulated. The scorecard, the chaos scheduler,
the pilot and the run are separate plans.

## Pre-registered predictions (Claude, 2026-11-07)

Written before any harness exists, so the report can score them:
- Week 1 passes. Deploys, onboarding and dashboards are where the platform is most hardened.
- The first SLO break has a physical cause (hub laptop sleep, an OrbStack update or a locked Keychain), not
  a logical one, and comes within 10 days unless the hub runs on hardware that cannot sleep.
- Operator minutes per day is the metric most likely to end the run, from about week 2.
- Credential and policy faults recover well. Node loss and disk pressure on the kine datastore recover
  poorly.
- Detection lags recovery: alerts are email-only, and nothing escalates out of hours.
- Overall: a 30–40% chance of passing the strict version on the first attempt.

## Scope

1. **Tenant model.** About 30 identities in the reference app's Keycloak realm, under a dedicated
   `saas-sim` group: 18 developers, 4 support, 3 product, 3 admins, 2 contractors. The user list is
   generated from a committed seed file (`scripts/etc/saas-sim/tenant.yaml`). Passwords come from Vault
   through ESO and are never written to git or to logs.
2. **Traffic.** A traffic profile for the existing loadtest tooling: a weekday daily cycle (low at night,
   peaks at 10:00 and 15:00 local time), lighter weekends, a small error-path share (bad logins, 404s).
   Peak rate is set as a fraction of the capacity measured by the loadtest baseline, never above 60% of
   it. The traffic targets only the simulation's own namespace and endpoints.
3. **Change load.** A scripted deploy driver produces 5–10 changes per weekday: config bumps,
   image-tag bumps of the reference app's sim build, and feature-flag toggles. Each goes through the
   normal GitOps path (branch → promotion gate → ArgoCD sync). Platform releases that merge during the
   run also count as change load.
4. **Operations calendar.** Routine work scheduled across 30 days in `scripts/etc/saas-sim/calendar.yaml`:
   3 onboardings, 2 offboardings (access must actually be revoked), 1 credential rotation per week, 1
   pinned-chart upgrade, 1 CVE remediation, 1 backup restore drill. Each item is filed as an ordinary
   `docs/bugs/` or task spec when it falls due, and handled through the normal dispatch workflow.
5. **Isolation.** All of it lives in the `saas-sim` namespace on the hub, plus its Keycloak group and its
   Vault path `secret/saas-sim/*`, under a read-only Vault policy for the app. Nothing here touches
   Hostinger, which receives only read-only external probes (plan 2).

## Non-goals

- No real customers, no real payment flows, no traffic to third-party services.
- No change to the reference app's code. The simulation drives it from outside.
- No new public ingress.
