# v1.40.0 — Hermes prior-art retrieval, and the evaluation that proves it works

**Target branch:** `k3d-manager-v1.40.0` (does not exist yet — do NOT cut it early, and do NOT work
on v1.39.0)
**Status:** SPEC. **Hard-blocked on v1.39.0 shipping WS1-WS3.** There is nothing to consume or
evaluate until the store, the indexer and `search()` exist.
**Scope:** WS4 (Hermes consumes retrieval), WS5 (the evaluation), and the share of WS6 that documents
them.

**Split from a single six-workstream spec on 2026-09-26** (operator approved). The original spec
carried WS1-WS6 and its own Risks section named the clean split: WS1 alone is a platform-component
release, WS2-WS5 the retrieval release. v1.39.0 took WS1-WS3
(`docs/plans/v1.39.0-vector-store-platform-and-retrieval.md`); this is the rest.

**Why the eval is here rather than with the store.** WS5 is the bulk of the effort, not a formality:
it requires hand-mining >=25 positive and >=25 hard-negative pairs from a corpus of **1,703 tracked
documents** (737 bugs, 451 issues, 435 plans, 80 retros, measured 2026-09-26 — the original spec's
"868" undercounted it). That cannot be compressed without making the measurement meaningless, and
pairing it with a live deployment in one release is what pushed the original spec over.

**This release also unblocks `slack-corpus-qa`.** That spec is hard-blocked on WS5's measured
recall@5. Land WS5 here and the two become implementable in the same release rather than one behind —
see `docs/plans/v1.40.0-slack-corpus-qa.md`.

## WS4 — Hermes consumes it (strictly additive)

**Hermes' filing decision does not change in this release.** The exact-slug glob at
`e2e_bugs.py:163` stays authoritative for `created` / `reopened` / `ongoing`.

What is added: when Hermes files a **new** bug doc, `_doc()` gains a `## Possible prior art` section
listing the top 3 retrieved documents with scores, or nothing when retrieval is unavailable.

This ordering is deliberate and is not negotiable within this release. An unproven retriever that
can suppress a bug report converts a false positive into a **silently unfiled defect** — the failure
mode is invisible, which is the worst kind. Additive output is reviewable by a human on every run,
and it generates the precision evidence needed to decide whether gating is ever safe.

Letting retrieval influence the filing decision requires its own spec, its own version, and a
measured precision floor. Do not do it here.

**Evidence that this ordering is right, from 2026-09-23.** The `alert_delivery` Hermes sensor was
specced, implemented and merged with 8 green tests, and its probe was inverted: it reported a total
alerting blackout against a healthy cluster (see WS5, "The eval must exercise the real component").
Had that sensor been wired to *act* rather than to report, it would have raised a blackout incident
for a cluster that was fine. An automated component that is confidently wrong on its first live run
is the normal case, not the pathological one — which is the whole argument for additive-only here.
Note also which direction the error took: the probe cried wolf. A retriever wired to suppress
filings fails the other way, silently, and would not have been caught at all.

## WS5 — Evaluation (gates WS4's future, and this release's claims)

- `scripts/tests/fixtures/doc-dedup/pairs.jsonl` — hand-mined duplicate/near-duplicate pairs from the
  existing corpus plus hard negatives (same component, genuinely different defect). Target ≥25
  positives and ≥25 hard negatives. Follow the schema style of
  `scripts/tests/fixtures/e2e-corpus/corpus.jsonl`, the existing precedent.
- Implement a **stdlib TF-IDF cosine scorer as the control** and measure both. If embeddings do not
  beat lexical on this corpus, that is a real finding and the deployed store earns its place on the
  platform-component argument alone — which is a legitimate outcome, but it must be *stated*, not
  quietly skipped.
- `scripts/tests/bin/test_find_similar_docs.py` — pytest, mirroring `test_check_doc_links.py`.
  Report **recall@5** for both scorers with a floor the suite enforces; break it out per directory,
  since `docs/bugs/` and `docs/issues/` dominate and plans/retros differ in register.
- Add the file to the `make test-pytest` target's path list.
- Mine the pairs **before** tuning either scorer, and never add a pair to fix a failing score.
- Record the measured numbers in the retro, not just in a passing test.
- **Seed pairs already mined** (2026-09-23, from the incident in "The filing-time gap"): the five
  istio-cni / CNI-dir documents form one near-duplicate cluster — 10 positive pairs, all of which the
  exact-slug gate provably misses. The pair
  (`2026-09-23-hostinger-registration-never-sets-provider-label`,
  `2026-06-08-thread-reply-triggers-unknown-command`) is a **hard negative**: they share the salient
  token `unknown` and nothing else. Include both; they are real, dated, and were not constructed to
  make a scorer look good.

### The eval must exercise the real component, not a stub

A defect on 2026-09-23 is the reason this subsection exists, and WS5 must not repeat it.
`scripts/tests/hermes/test_alert_delivery.py` had **8 green cases** covering the `alert_delivery`
sensor. The probe it consumes, `bin/k3dm-alert-delivery-status`, read the generated Alertmanager
Secret under the key `alertmanager.yaml`. That key does not exist — the Prometheus Operator emits
only `alertmanager.yaml.gz`, base64 **and** gzipped. The missing key produced `b64decode("") → b"" →
yaml.safe_load → None`, which collapsed to `root_receiver_is_null: true, child_routes: 0`: the probe
reported a **total alerting blackout against a healthy cluster with five working routes**.

All 8 tests stayed green, because they inject a stubbed `run` and the probe script never executes.
They proved the sensor's parsing of a hypothetical payload, not the probe's ability to produce one.
The suite measured the half of the seam that could not fail.

Binding consequences for WS5:

- The retrieval eval must run the **real indexer and the real scorer over real files on disk**. A
  test that feeds hand-written vectors or a stubbed embedding call measures the ranking arithmetic,
  which is not where this class of bug lives. Stub the hosted embeddings **network call** only, and
  when stubbed, assert against a **recorded real response**, not a synthesized one.
- At least one case must execute `bin/find-similar-docs` end to end as a subprocess against the real
  `docs/` tree, the way `test_check_doc_links.py` exercises its script.
- Every scorer assertion must be **mutation-proved**: break the scorer, confirm the recall floor goes
  red. A recall number that cannot fall is not a gate. This is the standing
  "a new test passing is not evidence it can fail" rule, and it applies to metrics too.
- Prefer a failure mode that is loud over one that is plausible. The blackout bug was survivable
  precisely because its wrong answer looked like a legitimate answer; a retriever returning zero
  results must be distinguishable from one that errored.

## Intended second consumer — Slack Q&A over the corpus (NOT this release)

Operator direction 2026-09-23: the Slack bot should consume this. It is the right second consumer
and it is deliberately **out of scope for v1.38.0**.

Why it is the right one:

- It proves WS3's `search()` is a shared library rather than a Hermes-private helper. A retrieval
  layer with exactly one caller has not been shown to be reusable; two callers is the cheapest
  possible proof, and Slack is a caller with a different query distribution (human, conversational,
  vocabulary-mismatched) than Hermes (machine, slug-derived). That difference is the whole point of
  semantic retrieval, so it is also the better source of precision evidence for the WS4 gating
  decision.
- The failure mode is benign. A Slack answer is read-only, advisory, and has a human reading it in
  the same second. Compare WS4, where a wrong suppression is silent. Shipping the forgiving consumer
  first and the unforgiving one later is the correct order.
- The transport already exists: `k3dm-slack-relay` (Cloudflare Worker), with
  `scripts/tests/plugins/slack_slash_commands.bats` and `slack_relay_ack.bats` already covering the
  slash-command and ack paths. This is a new handler, not new infrastructure.

Why not in v1.38.0:

- v1.38.0 already carries six workstreams, one of which (WS1) is a live deploy needing the operator's
  go. The spec's own Risks section says to split WS1 from WS2-WS5 rather than breach the 5-plan-doc
  cap; adding a seventh workstream argues the other way.
- It needs answers the release does not have yet. WS5 has not run, so the measured recall is unknown.
  Building a user-facing Q&A surface on an unmeasured retriever repeats the mistake WS4 exists to
  avoid — and this time the wrong answers would be read by a person who did not write the query
  expecting to audit it.
- Auth is unsolved for this shape. The existing relay handles slash commands with a static token;
  a Q&A bot reading the full docs corpus needs a decision about who may ask and what is returnable,
  which is a spec of its own, not a paragraph in this one.

Sequencing: land v1.38.0, run WS5, publish the measured recall@5 for both scorers, then spec the
Slack consumer in this same release with the measurement in hand — spec filed as `docs/plans/v1.40.0-slack-corpus-qa.md`. If WS5 shows embeddings do not beat the
lexical control, the Slack bot should use the control — and that finding is exactly why WS5 comes
first.

## What NOT to do

- Do **not** let retrieval gate, suppress or redirect Hermes' filing decision in this release.
- Do **not** tune either scorer before `pairs.jsonl` exists.
- Do **not** add a pair to `pairs.jsonl` to make a failing score pass.
- Do **not** accept a green suite as evidence the retriever works — see the `alert_delivery`
  precedent in WS5, where 8 green tests covered a probe that never executed.
- Do **not** add `torch`, `sentence-transformers`, `numpy`, or any third-party Python runtime
  dependency.
- Do **not** re-deploy or re-configure the vector store; WS1 shipped in v1.39.0 and re-running it is
  a live mutation that needs the operator.
- Do **not** index the out-of-repo `~/.claude/.../memory/` files here.
- Do **not** make the similarity check blocking, in the pre-commit hook or anywhere else.
- Do **not** create a PR, merge, commit to `main`, force-push, or use `--no-verify`.

## Risks

- **Hand-mining pairs is the bulk of the effort.** Reading enough of 1,188 bug and issue docs to
  label 25 genuine duplicates cannot be skipped without making the evaluation meaningless. Ten
  positive pairs and one hard negative are already mined (see WS5's seed list), so roughly 15
  positives and 24 negatives remain.
- **An unmeasured retriever looks like it works.** Plausible-looking top-5 results are the most
  likely way this ships broken. WS5 is the control, not paperwork.
- **Corpus skew.** Bugs and issues dominate; plans and retros differ in register and may score worse.
  Report recall per directory.
- **v1.39.0 ships the retriever unmeasured.** Until this release lands, `make find-similar-docs` is
  advisory output of unknown quality. If v1.40.0 slips, that caveat stays true and must stay in the
  guide.
